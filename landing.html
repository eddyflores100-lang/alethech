the property
what is un-concealed

This memory set forms part of a cryptographically verifiable history associated with a determined identity, whose commits can be independently verified with respect to their integrity, cryptographic authorship, and provenance relations.

When a reference checkpoint exists, it can additionally be verified that the presented history continues from that checkpoint.

two verdicts
what the verifier says
↻ click a card to flip the explanation

VALID_HISTORICAL

The commit is reachable from the cutoff head. Its signature verifies, its commit_id matches, its provenance chain is intact.

C ∈ ancestry(cutoff_head)
flip ↻

what it means

This memory was signed by the claimed identity before the key rotated, and the chain of custody from the checkpoint to now is unbroken.

NOT_IN_PROVEN_PRE_ROTATION_HISTORY

The commit exists, maybe even signs correctly — but it is not reachable from the cutoff head after rotation.

C ∉ ancestry(cutoff_head)
flip ↻

what it means

It could be a fabricated past, a rolled-back history, or a commit orphaned by rotation. The protocol refuses to attest continuity it cannot prove.

the checkpoint tower
An external checkpoint records a known-good head. On every verify, the protocol checks the current head is an ancestor of the checkpoint head — i.e. history moved forward, not backward.

Scroll to build the tower. A rolled-back checkpoint gets rejected, shakes, and turns red.

what it does NOT do
↻ click each to read the truth it refuses to claim
01

It does NOT prove that the agent's claims are true.

truth it refuses

Only that they were signed by the identity that claims them. Content-truth is the agent's responsibility, not the protocol's.

02

It does NOT detect rollback without an external checkpoint.

truth it refuses

Without a reference checkpoint emitted and stored elsewhere, the protocol cannot know what "forward" means.

03

It does NOT encrypt content at rest.

truth it refuses

Commits are integrity-protected, not confidentiality-protected. Use a separate encryption layer if you need secrecy.

04

It does NOT delegate permissions between agents.

truth it refuses

There is no ACL, no role graph, no capability transfer. Each identity is sovereign over its own signed history.

05

It does NOT call any LLM.

truth it refuses

Zero network, zero inference, zero "interpretation". Pure local cryptography. The verifier is deterministic.

06

It does NOT prove physical signing time.

truth it refuses

Only the timestamp recorded in the artifact. Wall-clock truth is outside the cryptographic scope.
