§ 01 — the property

what does it mean to un-conceal?

This memory set forms part of a cryptographically verifiable history associated with a determined identity, whose commits can be independently verified with respect to their integrity, cryptographic authorship, and provenance relations.


When a reference checkpoint exists, it can additionally be verified that the presented history continues from that checkpoint.

The protocol does NOT prove that the agent's claims are true — only that they were signed by the identity that claims them. The truth being un-concealed is the truth about transmission integrity, not about content.

§ 02 — the mathematics

four equations, one guarantee

commit identity
The commit's identifier is the SHA-256 hash of its canonical JSON (per RFC 8785), excluding the commit_id and signature fields. Changing any content changes the hash. The hash IS the identity.
signature
The signature authenticates the object plus its commit_id. This binds the signature to the exact bytes that produced the hash. Tampering with content breaks the hash; tampering with the hash breaks the signature.
agent identity
The agent's identity is cryptographically derived from its public key. You cannot claim an agent_id without controlling the corresponding Ed25519 private key. Identity is not declared — it is derived.
reachability
A commit signed with a revoked key is accepted only if it is reachable from the cutoff_head — the causal frontier at the moment of key rotation. If not reachable: NOT_IN_PROVEN_PRE_ROTATION_HISTORY.
§ 03 — the architecture

a Merkle DAG of signed memory

Commit D is signed with the revoked key-001, but its parent is C₂ (the cutoff_head). Since D is a child of C₂, not an ancestor, the reachability check fails. D is rejected as NOT_IN_PROVEN_PRE_ROTATION_HISTORY.

§ 04 — the verdicts

two verdicts, no temporal claims

When a commit is signed with a revoked key, the verifier can only demonstrate one of two things. It deliberately does NOT claim to prove when the commit was created.

VALID_HISTORICAL
C ∈ ancestry(cutoff_head)
The commit IS a member of the ancestry covered by the rotation's cutoff_head. The signature is valid, and the commit belongs to the causal history the rotation closed. This is the legitimate historical case — the commit was made before rotation, and the cutoff_head includes it in its history.
NOT_IN_PROVEN_PRE_ROTATION_HISTORY
C ∉ ancestry(cutoff_head)
The commit is NOT a member of ancestry(cutoff_head). The verifier cannot prove it belongs to the causal history the rotation closed. This is not a claim that the commit was "created after rotation" — that would require a clock the protocol does not have. It is strictly a membership verdict.
§ 05 — anti-rollback

monotonic sequence, not system clock

A correctly-signed but old checkpoint could be presented as current. The system clock is NOT the authority. The sequence field is.

§ 07 — honesty

what it does NOT do

NOT prove claims are true

Only that they were signed by the identity that claims them.

NOT detect rollback without checkpoint

External checkpoint required for continuity verification.

NOT encrypt content at rest

Integrity ≠ confidentiality. A signature does not encrypt.

NOT delegate permissions

No inter-agent authorization. Each identity is sovereign.

NOT call any LLM

Zero-LLM by design. Bytes and cryptographic properties only.

NOT prove physical signing time

Only the timestamp recorded in the artifact. No clock authority.
